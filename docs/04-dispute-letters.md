# Dispute Letters and How to Send Them

## What a dispute letter actually is

A dispute letter is not anger on paper. It is a short, polite, dated record
that says: *"Please recheck this specific claim against these specific
documents."* It creates a paper trail, gives the billing office something
actionable, and — quietly — tells everyone that you keep records.

You never need to threaten, cite code sections, or prove anything. You need to
describe the mismatch and ask for a review. Most disputes end in a correction,
a refund, or a clear explanation you didn't have before.

> **Scope reminder:** this is admin help only — no legal advice. If a dispute
> involves very large amounts, collections, or anything you'd want a
> professional on, this guide's chapter 5 templates are still your first step,
> but a consumer-protection resource or attorney is the right next stop.

## Before you write

You need exactly three things (all from your folder / claims log):

1. **Claim number** or billing account number (from the bill or EOB).
2. **Date of service** in question.
3. **One-sentence description of the mismatch** — e.g. "billed twice,"
   "I was not seen on that date," "EOB says my share is $50 but the bill says $120."

## Pick the right template

Full copy-paste versions live in
[worksheets/dispute-letter-templates.md](worksheets/dispute-letter-templates.md):

| Template | Use when |
| --- | --- |
| **T1 — General review request** | The numbers don't match and you want a recheck |
| **T2 — Duplicate charge** | Same service appears twice |
| **T3 — Service you did not receive** | Date or service doesn't match reality |
| **T4 — Out-of-network / surprise charge** | In-network visit, out-of-network charge |
| **T5 — Financial-assistance request** | You can't afford an amount (pairs with Chapter 5) |

## The shape of a good letter (any of them)

1. **Header block:** your name, address, phone; the provider or insurer name
   and address; the date you send it; and a one-line subject with the claim
   number and date of service.
2. **The facts, in three sentences:** what visit, what the document says, what
   doesn't add up.
3. **The ask:** one specific thing — review, correct, reprocess, refund.
4. **Your paperwork promise:** " Copies of the bill and EOB are enclosed."
   (Only enclose *copies*, never your only originals.)
5. **The close:** one question with a deadline ("Could you reply within 30 days?"),
   your signature, and a line saying how they can reach you.
6. Optionally: a short list of attachments and a reminder that you keep logs.

## How to send it (the boring details matter)

- **Postal mail with a tracking number** is the gold standard — cheap, quiet,
  and gives both sides a record.
- Also email or portal-message a copy when a portal exists; it speeds things up.
- Keep the send date in your [claims log](07-claims-log.md). That single date
  doubles as your "if nothing happens in 30 days, follow up" reminder.

## Sample: T1, filled in with pretend details

> Jane Doe, 44 Woodside Rd, Springfield
> To: Billing Department, Maple Family Clinic
> Date: March 12, 2026
> Re: Claim 2026-014822, date of service February 3, 2026
>
> Hello — I'm writing to ask for a review of the bill for my February 3 visit.
> My EOB shows my share as $50, but the bill lists $120 due.
> Could you compare the claim against the EOB and send a corrected statement?
> Copies of both documents are enclosed. I'd appreciate a reply within 30 days.
> Thank you for your time — I know billing is a busy department.
> Sincerely, Jane Doe (555-0100)

That's the entire genre: short, specific, dated, kind, and askable.

## If the first letter doesn't fix it

1. **Wait the 30 days** (the letter asked for this politely).
2. **One follow-up**, same channel: "Following up on my letter of March 12..."
3. **Member services** of your insurer, with the claim number handy — use the
   [phone scripts](08-phone-scripts.md).
4. Insurer-side appeal path: your plan documents list one; a dispute letter to
   the *insurer* ("I'm requesting a review/appeal of claim X") mirrors the same
   shape as the templates here.
5. Log every step. Ten rows in a claims log is what turns "this
   disagreement has dragged on" into "here is the timeline."

## Fair-warning sentences you can borrow

- "I'd like to resolve this before it goes to collections, please let me know if more information is needed."
- "Could you confirm what you received and who's assigned to it, so I can follow up?"
- "Could you note today's date on the account when this is reviewed?"
