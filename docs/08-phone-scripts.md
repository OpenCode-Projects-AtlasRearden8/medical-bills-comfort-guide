# Phone Scripts (Word-for-Word)

## How to use this page

Phone calls about billing sound scarier than they are — the person on the
other end does this all day and generally wants a resolvable question. Each
script below is a complete speaking template: fill in the brackets, read it
aloud if that helps, and glance at the
[call-notes worksheet](worksheets/call-notes-worksheet.md) while you talk.

General rhythm that works everywhere:
**identify → describe → one specific ask → confirm in writing → note the time.**

Treat the person kindly; they are decreasers of your problem, not the source
of it. And when they say "hold, please," that is normal — use printable
worksheets or a quiet five-minute trick (tea, stretch) while you wait.

## Script 1 — Member services (your insurer)

> "Hi, my name is [name], insured under member ID [number]. I have a question
> about a claim: number [claim], date of service [date], from [provider].
> Could you tell me: what was the allowed amount, what did the plan pay, and
> how much is my share? ... And could I get that repeated slowly so I can
> write it? ... Thank you — can you note the account that I called today?"

**Why it works:** it's three factual questions and a request for a note.
Nothing adversarial.

## Script 2 — Provider billing office (questions about a bill)

> "Hi, I'm calling about a bill for account [number], date of service [date].
> I have the EOB in front of me, and my share per the EOB is [$X], but the
> bill says [$Y]. Could you check the claim against my insurance
> explanation? ... Should I send that in writing too, and to what address?
> Could I get your name so I can mention it if I write in?"

**Follow-up line,** if the numbers puzzle you both:
> "Could you reprocess or resubmit the claim if something was out of sync?"

## Script 3 — Requesting an itemized bill

> "Hello, could you send an itemized bill for account [number]? That's the
> line-by-line version with codes and charges, not just the summary. What's your
> mailing address or portal for that? ... Thank you — could you note the
> account that I requested it today?"

People frequently request itemized bills; it is completely standard.

## Script 4 — Financial assistance screening

> "Hi, I'd like to ask about financial assistance for account [number].
> First question: does the provider have a financial-assistance or
> charity-care policy? ... What would I need to send, and is there a
> deadline? ... While it's being reviewed, does the bill pause? ... Could
> you confirm those three answers in writing or on the account?"

(This is the ten-questions list from
[Chapter 5](05-financial-assistance.md) compressed, so the top three get
asked on the first call.)

## Script 5 — Payment plan

> "I'd like to set up a payment plan for account [number]. I can pay [$X] a
> month. Could you confirm whether there's any interest or fee, how long the
> plan runs, and the first due date? ... Is it fine if the first payment is
> [date]? ... Could you send the terms in writing?"

And the early-warning version, if a payment will be late:
> "Heads up — I might be a few days late this month due to [timing]. Could we
> move the due date to [date] for this month only?"

## Script 6 — Collections call (short, controlled)

> "Let me make sure I have the details right: account [number] with
> [provider], for services on [date], for the amount of [$X] — is that
> correct? ... I'm resolving this with [provider]'s billing office, or I
> intend to. Could I contact them first and call you back with an update,
> and what is your reference number?"

You're allowed to merely *gather* information and call back later. You are
also allowed to say: "Please put any further contact in writing." Note
everything in the call-notes worksheet.

## When the call stalls

- "Could you transfer me to someone who handles [billing disputes /
  financial assistance]?"
- "What's the best way to put this in writing so it's attached to the account?"
- "Could we do this by mail or portal instead?" — often yes, and writing is
  your friend anyway.
- If you're sure of nothing else: **"I'll follow up in writing. Thank you for
  your time."** Then it goes in the log, and the log quietly does the rest.

End every call by noting: date, time, name of person, one-line summary, next
step. Thirty seconds, and the phone holds no power over you anymore.

*One more scope note, with warmth: none of the words in these scripts are
legal or medical advice — they're just the plain-English sentences that
billing professionals actually respond well to.*
