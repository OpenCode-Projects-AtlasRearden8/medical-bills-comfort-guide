# Deductible and Out-of-Pocket, with Big Easy Numbers

*Admin help only — buckets and round numbers below are illustrative, never
advice about your real plan. Your plan documents are the source of truth.*

## The bucket picture

Health insurance math is much easier with buckets. Imagine three buckets on
your shelf, all empty on January 1st:

1. **Deductible bucket** — you fill it alone.
2. **Sharing bucket** — you fill it together with insurance.
3. **Out-of-pocket-maximum bucket** — when it's full, insurance pays
   everything for the rest of the year (for covered services).

We'll use small pretend numbers all year — say your plan is:

- Deductible: **$500**
- Coinsurance after that: you pay **20%**, insurance pays **80%**
- Out-of-pocket maximum: **$2,000**

## Bucket 1: the deductible

Before your insurance pays anything toward most services, you pay out of your
own pocket up to $500.

**Example with round numbers.** You get a $150 allowed-amount bill:

- Deductible used so far: $0 of $500.
- You pay the full $150. Deductible now at $150.
- Next visit's allowed amount is $400? You'd pay the remaining $350 of your
  deductible... and you're done: $500 total reached.

> **Check your EOB's wording.** If it shows the full allowed amount under
> "your responsibility" early in the year, that's usually the deductible
> talking — and it's expected, not an error.

## Bucket 2: the sharing (coinsurance) bucket

Once the deductible is met, you pay your coinsurance *share of the allowed
amount*, not of the sticker price.

**Example.** Deductible is met. An allowed amount of $300 arrives with 20%
coinsurance:

- Plan discount: none needed — allowed amount is the price now.
- You pay: 20% × $300 = **$60**.
- Insurance pays: 80% × $300 = **$240**.

Your annual total paid so far still counts toward the out-of-pocket maximum:
$500 (deductible) + $60 = $560.

## Bucket 3: the out-of-pocket maximum

Same 20% pattern continues until your year's total hits $2,000. After that,
the insurer pays 100% of the allowed amount for covered services for the rest
of the year. Scenario check with our pretend plan:

| Total allowed charges this year | You pay | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $150 | $150 | Filling the deductible |
| $500 | $500 | Deductible now exactly met |
| $1,500 | $700 | $500 deductible + 20% of the next $1,000 |
| $7,500 | $2,000 | Hits the maximum, then $0 |

That is the whole system. **Three buckets, filled in order.**

## Copays live a little outside the line

Some plans collect a flat **copay** at each visit (for example $50) that does
not wait for the deductible. It still counts toward the out-of-pocket maximum.
Your EOB usually shows the copay as its own line — if you see both a copay and
20% on the same service lines, that is the plan design, not double-billing.
When unsure, that is a perfect member-services question
([phone scripts](08-phone-scripts.md)).

## The two magic numbers on your card

Your insurance card (or your plan's website) lists your real versions of:

- **DeD** — deductible (individual / family)
- **OOPM** — out-of-pocket maximum (individual / family)

Write them at the top of your [claims log](07-claims-log.md). Every EOB
tells you how much of each is "used" — which means you can track your buckets
with one line per claim. That is the entire bookkeeping system of this guide.

## Round-number self-check

Take any EOB and confirm: *your responsibility* = deductible-share + copay +
coinsurance-share for that claim. Example: $200 allowed, $100 deductible left,
20% coinsurance — you pay $100 (deductible) + 20%×$100 ($20) = **$120**, and
insurer pays $80. If your math and the EOB disagree by more than a rounding,
something is off — head to
[Chapter 3: Common Billing Errors](03-common-billing-errors.md).
