# Payment Plans Without Stress

*Admin help only — no financial or legal advice here, just paperwork
patterns that billing offices handle every day.*

## What a payment plan is

A payment plan is an arrangement to pay a bill over several months. It is one
of the most common tools in medical billing — providers offer them daily —
and agreeing to one is a completely ordinary, unremarkable thing to do. There
is no shame in it and no drama in it. It is a calendar, not a confession.

**Scope reminder:** admin help only. This chapter does not cover financing
through third-party loans or credit products; it covers plans you arrange
directly with the provider, which are usually simpler and interest-free.

## Before you call: your two numbers

With your bill and [claims log](07-claims-log.md) in front of you, know:

1. **Total owed** (after any insurance adjustments — see
   [Chapter 1](01-bill-vs-eob.md)).
2. **What you can actually pay monthly, comfortably.** Take a low, sustainable
   number. A plan you keep beats a plan you miss. If $25 is your number, $25
   is your number.

## The five questions to ask (works on the phone or by mail)

1. **"Do you offer payment plans?"** (Almost always yes.)
2. **"Is there interest or a fee?"** Hospital plans are usually 0%.
   Confirm, in those words.
3. **"What's the minimum monthly payment, and can it be lower?"**
   Ask specifically for a lower number than offered. Sometimes yes, sometimes no.
4. **"Does this pause collections while it's active?"** Confirm and note the date.
5. **"Can you confirm the terms in writing?"** Amount, duration, first due
   date. Paper or portal message is perfect.
   Use the [call-notes worksheet](worksheets/call-notes-worksheet.md) as you go.

## The three-sentence pattern for phone calls

State the situation, state your number, ask for the terms:

> "I'd like to set up a payment plan for account [number]. I can pay
> $50 a month. Could you confirm no interest, how many payments, and send
> the terms in writing?"

That's a complete negotiation. If the offered terms don't work, say so
calmly: "That's more than I can sustain — could we look at a lower amount or
a longer timeline?"

## Keeping it on the rails

- Put the due date somewhere visible (calendar, phone reminder). Plans usually
  bill automatically or send a statement — know which kind yours is.
- Mark each payment in your claims log. Seeing the row shrink the balance is
  genuinely calming.
- **If you'll miss a payment, call *before* the due date.** A one-sentence
  heads-up call is almost always fine: "I'm a few days off this month — can we
  push it to the 20th?" Skipping silently is what causes problems; notifying
  early almost never does.
- When the plan ends, ask for a **zero-balance statement** for your folder.

## When a plan and assistance overlap

If you apply for financial assistance
([Chapter 5](05-financial-assistance.md)) while a plan exists, tell the
billing office. Approved assistance is usually applied to the remaining
balance; ask: "If assistance is approved, how does that change my plan?"

## One-page summary of this chapter

- Deal only with the provider, in your own words, at your own number.
- Confirm: no interest, total duration, pause-on-collections.
- Get terms in writing; log every payment.
- Call before you fall behind, not after.
