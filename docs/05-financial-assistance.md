# Financial Assistance: Questions to Ask

## The premise of this chapter

Hospitals and many clinics have financial-assistance and charity-care
programs, and they are usually *not* advertised loudly. Asking about them is
normal, expected, and has nothing to do with the quality of your care. Billing
staff answer these questions every day. This chapter is a checklist of which
questions to ask, and where the answers land in your paperwork.

**This is admin help only — not financial, legal, or medical advice.** It's a
list of questions, not a promise of outcomes. Programs vary by provider and by
state.

## The ten questions, in the order that works

Ask these to the provider's billing office (the number printed on the bill):

1. **"Do you have a financial-assistance or charity-care policy?"**
   Nonprofit hospitals are generally required to have one in writing; ask for
   a copy or where to find it.
2. **"What income (and household size) do you look at?"** Usually it's a
   multiple of the federal poverty level — you don't need to know the level,
   just give honest income and household size and let them screen.
3. **"Does the policy apply to this bill or to future care?"** Sometimes
   existing bills qualify for discounts, sometimes only the current year.
4. **"What documents do you need, and what's the deadline?"**
   Typically: a short application, proof of income (tax return or a couple of
   pay stubs). Deadlines matter — ask before any due date on the bill.
5. **"Does applying pause collection activity?"** Many policies pause
   billing/collections while an application is pending. Confirm and write down
   the date you applied.
6. **"Can this be combined with a payment plan?"** If assistance only covers
   a percentage, the remainder can often go on a plan
   ([Chapter 6](06-payment-plans.md)).
7. **"Is there a prompt-pay or self-pay discount that's simpler than the full application?"**
   Sometimes a smaller quick discount is available without paperwork.
8. **"Can you confirm this in writing?"** Payment arrangements, discounts,
   and pause-on-collections should exist on paper (or as a portal message).
9. **"Who do I contact if my situation changes?"** Assistance programs can
   usually be re-checked if income drops.
10. **"What happens after I'm approved — revised bill or credit?"** You want
    to know which document will reflect the change so your claims log stays tidy.

## Where the questions fit into your paperwork system

- Write every call into your
  [call-notes worksheet](worksheets/call-notes-worksheet.md) — date, name,
  what was said, next step.
- Log the application as a row in your
  [claims log worksheet](worksheets/claims-log-worksheet.md), like any claim.
- If a yardstick helps you decide whether to ask: as one rough pattern, many
  hospital policies give meaningful discounts well below certain incomes and
  none above a ceiling — which is exactly why *asking* beats guessing. The
  application is usually one page. The worst realistic outcome is "not
  eligible," and you've still lost nothing.

## Mnemonic for the whole chapter

**A-P-P: Ask, Paper, Pause.**
**Ask** the ten questions. Everything goes on **Paper** (or a portal message).
Applying usually **Pauses** collections while pending.

And if cost is the blocker on any *decision about care*, say that to your
provider's office (not this manual's lane, but worth knowing: billing staff
and care teams often have options they can't offer unless cost comes up).
This manual stays on the paperwork side of that line.
